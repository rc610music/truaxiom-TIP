# SYS-0001 — Organizational Brain

**Document ID:** SYS-0001  
**Status:** Draft v1.0  
**Sprint:** Sprint 001 — Foundation  
**System Type:** Core Platform System  
**Owner:** TruaXiom Intelligence Platform (TIP)

---

## 1. Purpose

The Organizational Brain is the interpretive intelligence layer of TIP.

Its role is to transform stored organizational knowledge into usable context, meaning, judgment, and recommendations. It sits between the Knowledge Graph and the Intelligence Engine.

The Knowledge Graph stores reality. The Organizational Brain interprets that reality. The Intelligence Engine decides what should happen next.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Most AI tools operate from isolated prompts, short-lived chat history, or disconnected documents. That creates repeated context setup, shallow recommendations, duplicate content, and fragmented operations.

A business does not need another isolated assistant. It needs a system that understands the organization as a living entity.

The Organizational Brain solves this by maintaining an interpreted model of:

- what the organization is,
- what it believes,
- what it sells,
- what it is building,
- what it has already published,
- what its customers need,
- what its systems know,
- what has worked,
- what has failed,
- and what should happen next.

---

## 3. Responsibilities

The Organizational Brain is responsible for:

1. Interpreting the Knowledge Graph.
2. Providing organizational context to modules and agents.
3. Maintaining the organization's strategic memory.
4. Detecting gaps, conflicts, duplication, and stale knowledge.
5. Supporting recommendation generation.
6. Supporting decision explanation.
7. Enforcing brand, voice, product, and operational context.
8. Connecting historical decisions to current actions.
9. Helping Mission Control surface what matters most.
10. Keeping TIP aligned with the organization's real-world goals.

---

## 4. Non-Responsibilities

The Organizational Brain does not:

- store raw documents directly,
- replace the Knowledge Graph,
- execute workflows,
- publish content,
- authenticate users,
- manage billing,
- deploy applications,
- or make final founder-level decisions.

Those responsibilities belong to other systems.

---

The Organizational Brain reads from:

## 6. Core Outputs

The Organizational Brain produces:

- context packets,
- recommendations,
- priority signals,
- conflict alerts,
- freshness scores,
- content gap assessments,
- relationship explanations,
- product summaries,
- brand-context instructions,
- decision rationale,
- and Mission Control insights.

---

## 7. Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

The Knowledge Graph is the canonical model of organizational knowledge.

The Organizational Brain does not own the truth. It interprets the truth.

Example:

```text
Knowledge Graph:
RootWork has articles about anxiety, burnout, breathwork, and journaling.

Organizational Brain:
RootWork has strong introductory wellness coverage but lacks connected practice assets, downloadable resources, and internal link pathways for burnout recovery.
```

---

## 8. Relationship to the Intelligence Engine

The Intelligence Engine asks the Organizational Brain for context before deciding what action to take.

Example flow:

```text
Intelligence Engine receives objective:
Improve RootWork content ecosystem.

Organizational Brain returns:
- Existing content map
- Topic coverage gaps
- Brand voice constraints
- Related products
- Recent decisions
- Confidence signals

Intelligence Engine decides:
Generate a burnout recovery resource package and queue it for review.
```

---

## 9. Organizational DNA

The Organizational Brain maintains a structured interpretation of the organization's DNA.

Organizational DNA includes:

- Mission
- Vision
- Values
- Brand voice
- Founder philosophy
- Target audience
- Product principles
- Offer structure
- Design language
- Competitive posture
- Ethical boundaries
- Strategic priorities

Organizational DNA becomes the default context for every module.

---

## 10. Context Packets

A context packet is a structured set of information generated by the Organizational Brain for a specific task.

Example context packet:

```json
{
  "organization": "RootWork",
  "brand_voice": "grounded, healing, inclusive, practical",
  "objective": "expand burnout support content",
  "known_assets": ["burnout article", "breathwork practice", "journal prompt"],
  "gaps": ["downloadable resource", "email sequence", "internal links"],
  "constraints": ["avoid fear-based language", "keep tone supportive"],
  "recommended_action": "create burnout starter guide",
  "confidence": 0.91
}
```

---

## 11. Memory Model

The Organizational Brain uses layered memory:

### Strategic Memory
Long-term mission, vision, positioning, and product strategy.

### Operational Memory
Current projects, sprints, statuses, tasks, workflows, and active constraints.

### Content Memory
Published assets, topics, formats, internal links, campaigns, and content gaps.

### Decision Memory
Architecture decisions, founder approvals, rejected ideas, rationale, and version history.

### Performance Memory
Metrics, outcomes, experiments, conversion data, traffic changes, and engagement trends.

---

## 12. Confidence and Provenance

Every interpretation should include:

- source references,
- confidence score,
- freshness status,
- last reviewed timestamp,
- and whether human approval is required.

TIP should never treat all knowledge as equally trustworthy.

## 14. Success Criteria

SYS-0001 is successful when:

- TIP can generate recommendations without requiring repeated prompt context.
- Mission Control can summarize organizational state from structured knowledge.
- Agents can receive task-specific context packets.
- Decisions are explainable.
- Duplicate or conflicting organizational knowledge can be identified.
- RootWork can be connected as the first production client.

---

## 15. Initial Implementation Target

The first implementation of the Organizational Brain will support RootWork.

Initial RootWork functions:

- understand published content,
- map topics to resources and practices,
- identify content gaps,
- recommend next additions,
- preserve RootWork brand voice,
- and prepare structured publishing suggestions.
